Based on the base paper's own reference list (retrieved via CrossRef), CoWiC-MAC belongs to the **Cross-Technology Communication (CTC) and WiFi/low-power-radio coexistence** research line: it addresses coordinated medium access between WiFi and heterogeneous low-power wireless devices (e.g., ZigBee/IEEE 802.15.4-class radios) sharing the 2.4 GHz ISM band, building on the authors' own prior CTC work (WibZig, IPSN 2023; WAIC-CTC, Computer Networks 2025). The literature survey below is therefore scoped to WiFi-centric MAC design, WiFi/ZigBee coexistence, cross-technology communication, and coordinated/explicit channel-access mechanisms (including IEEE 802.11ax high-efficiency MAC features), which are the technical neighborhoods the base paper itself draws on.

## 4. Literature Survey — Paragraph Format

**Bianchi (2000)** presented the foundational analytical model of the IEEE 802.11 Distributed Coordination Function (DCF), the CSMA/CA-based MAC at the core of WiFi. The proposed approach models the binary exponential backoff process as a two-dimensional discrete-time Markov chain. It uses this chain to derive closed-form saturation throughput expressions for both the Basic Access and RTS/CTS mechanisms, addressing the lack of an accurate, tractable performance model for contention-based WiFi channel access. The results showed the model closely matches simulated throughput and that RTS/CTS reduces collision cost as contention and frame size grow. The main advantage of this approach is its enduring analytical foundation, reused across nearly all later 802.11 MAC studies, while a limitation is its restriction to saturated, homogeneous, ideal-channel scenarios with no heterogeneous-technology coexistence modeled.

**Jamieson and Balakrishnan (2007)** proposed PPR (Partial Packet Recovery), a cross-layer scheme addressing the inefficiency of discarding an entire corrupted WiFi frame after a single bit error. The proposed approach lets the receiver identify which chip/bit segments of a frame were received correctly using PHY-layer confidence information. It uses this segment-level information to request retransmission of only the corrupted portions rather than the whole frame, addressing wasted airtime in lossy or interference-prone channels. The results showed substantial throughput gains over standard full-frame ARQ as loss rates increased. The main advantage of this approach is fine-grained, airtime-efficient recovery without changing higher-layer protocols, while a limitation is its dependence on PHY-layer confidence-estimation hardware support.

**Huang et al. (2010)** presented a scheme exploiting WiFi's temporal white space to protect ZigBee performance in shared 2.4 GHz deployments. The proposed approach senses and predicts idle intervals between WiFi transmissions and schedules ZigBee transmissions into those predicted gaps. It uses passive WiFi traffic observation rather than any WiFi-side protocol modification, addressing ZigBee's vulnerability to WiFi interference without requiring WiFi cooperation. The results showed markedly improved ZigBee delivery under heavy co-channel WiFi load. The main advantage of this approach is that it is fully WiFi-transparent and deployable unilaterally, while a limitation is degraded prediction accuracy under bursty or unpredictable WiFi traffic, since coordination is one-way rather than negotiated.

**Liang et al. (2010)** developed a forward-error-correction coding scheme to help low-power ZigBee networks survive WiFi interference. The proposed approach adds structured, burst-loss-aware redundancy tailored to the characteristic corruption pattern WiFi interference imposes on ZigBee frames. It uses this coding at the ZigBee transmitter/receiver pair alone, addressing packet loss without requiring any WiFi-side cooperation or protocol coordination. The results showed markedly higher ZigBee packet reception rates than uncoded transmission under representative WiFi interference. The main advantage of this approach is its unilateral deployability on resource-constrained ZigBee nodes, while a limitation is that the added redundancy reduces effective payload throughput and energy efficiency.

**Zhang and Shin (2011)** presented a coexistence mechanism enabling heterogeneous WiFi and ZigBee devices to detect and align around each other's channel activity. The proposed approach relies on PHY-layer energy/activity detection rather than a shared MAC protocol between the two technologies. It uses this detection to time transmissions so as to minimize mutual interference, addressing the general problem of coexistence between technologically incompatible radios in the same ISM band. The results showed substantially reduced collision-induced loss for both technologies relative to uncoordinated coexistence. The main advantage of this approach is that it requires no protocol interoperability between radios, while a limitation is that implicit, detection-based coordination is less precise and less scalable than explicit signaling.

**Wang et al. (2014)** presented WiCop, a scheme engineering WiFi temporal white-spaces for safety-critical wireless body-area network (WBAN) traffic in medical settings. The proposed approach passively models WiFi's traffic occupancy pattern and schedules WBAN transmissions into predicted white spaces with worst-case latency guarantees. It uses this real-time white-space prediction to address the need for deterministic, bounded-latency medical sensor communication despite coexisting WiFi traffic. The results showed bounded worst-case access latency achievable for medical WBAN traffic sharing spectrum with unmodified WiFi. The main advantage of this approach is its deterministic safety guarantee, stronger than best-effort coexistence, while a limitation is reliance on accurate short-term WiFi traffic prediction, which weakens under highly dynamic or multi-AP conditions.

**Guo et al. (2014)** proposed a real-time adaptive coding scheme to enhance ZigBee throughput under WiFi interference. The proposed approach dynamically adjusts forward-error-correction redundancy based on online estimation of current WiFi interference intensity. It uses this adaptive redundancy to address the throughput penalty of always-on, worst-case coding, balancing reliability and efficiency as interference varies. The results showed the adaptive scheme outperforming both static-redundancy coding and uncoded transmission across varying WiFi loads. The main advantage of this approach is interference-proportional efficiency rather than fixed conservative coding, while a limitation is the added computational and energy overhead of continuous interference estimation on constrained ZigBee nodes.

**Yang et al. (2016)** presented a cross-technology interference mitigation approach for WiFi–ZigBee coexistence networks. The proposed approach builds an empirical, statistical model of cross-technology interference and pairs it with interference-aware channel/timing selection and adaptive transmission. It uses this combined characterization-plus-mitigation strategy to address both the detection and avoidance of cross-technology collisions, rather than tolerating interference passively. The results showed substantially improved ZigBee packet reception ratio and fewer retransmissions under realistic interference. The main advantage of this approach is combining active mitigation with interference modeling for greater robustness, while a limitation is that model calibration is specific to the studied technology pair and interference regime.

**Bellalta (2016)** presented a survey of the IEEE 802.11ax amendment's high-efficiency WLAN mechanisms. The proposed overview covers OFDMA-based multi-user access, MU-MIMO, and spatial reuse via BSS coloring, targeting efficiency in dense, overlapping-BSS deployments. It uses a descriptive/tutorial methodology rather than new experiments, addressing the need to consolidate emerging 802.11ax MAC/PHY features for the research community. The results highlighted OFDMA scheduling and spatial reuse as the key contributors to efficiency gains, with legacy-device coexistence flagged as an open challenge. The main advantage of this approach is providing an accessible, comprehensive reference on 802.11ax's coordinated-access toolbox, while a limitation is the absence of original experimental validation.

**Li and He (2017)** proposed WEBee, a physical-layer Cross-Technology Communication (CTC) technique enabling direct WiFi-to-ZigBee communication via emulation. The proposed approach manipulates only the payload of standard WiFi OFDM packets so that a high-speed WiFi radio emulates the desired waveform of a low-speed ZigBee radio. It uses this PHY-level emulation to address the low throughput (tens of bps) of earlier packet-level-modulation CTC schemes, requiring no hardware or firmware changes. The results showed more than 99% reliable parallel CTC at 126 Kbps in noisy environments — about 16,000× faster than prior CTC. The main advantage of this approach is zero-cost, commodity-compatible deployment with dramatically higher CTC throughput, while a limitation is that emulation fidelity depends on precise WiFi-transmitter timing/waveform control.

**Deng et al. (2017)** presented a review of IEEE 802.11ax mechanisms framed around supporting intelligent, heterogeneous information infrastructure. The proposed overview examines OFDMA-based random access, trigger-frame-based coordinated uplink scheduling, and spatial reuse. It uses a descriptive methodology to address how 802.11ax MAC design must adapt as dense, heterogeneous IoT-style device populations increasingly share WLAN infrastructure. The results argued that trigger-frame-coordinated scheduling and spatial reuse are essential for sustaining efficiency as device heterogeneity and density grow. The main advantage of this approach is explicitly framing 802.11ax MAC evolution around heterogeneous-device coexistence, while a limitation is the lack of new quantitative evaluation.

**Yin et al. (2018)** presented an approach using Cross-Technology Communication for explicit channel coordination, moving beyond opportunistic interference avoidance. The proposed approach lets heterogeneous devices, such as WiFi and ZigBee nodes, directly exchange channel-access intent and negotiate transmission windows via CTC signaling. It uses this explicit negotiation to address the imprecision and unfairness of implicit, sensing-based coexistence schemes. The results showed reduced cross-technology collisions and improved channel utilization and fairness relative to implicit coexistence. The main advantage of this approach is active, negotiated coordination rather than passive avoidance — conceptually the closest prior work to CoWiC-MAC's coordinated design goal — while a limitation is the airtime consumed by CTC signaling overhead itself.

**Khorov et al. (2019)** presented a comprehensive tutorial and evaluation of IEEE 802.11ax's high-efficiency WLAN mechanisms. The proposed work analytically and experimentally evaluates OFDMA, MU-MIMO, spatial reuse/BSS coloring, and Target Wake Time together. It uses combined analytical and simulation methodology to address how these mechanisms interact in dense and heterogeneous WLAN deployments, rather than evaluating them in isolation. The results showed that combining OFDMA with spatial reuse and TWT yields the greatest efficiency gains, though sensitive to legacy-device presence and traffic mix. The main advantage of this approach is its rigor and completeness as a coordinated-access MAC design reference, while a limitation is that evaluation stays within the 802.11 ecosystem rather than cross-radio-technology heterogeneity.

**Nurchis and Bellalta (2019)** presented Target Wake Time (TWT), a scheduled-access mechanism introduced in IEEE 802.11ax. The proposed approach lets the access point negotiate individual wake/access windows with stations, converting part of channel access from contention-based to AP-coordinated scheduling. It uses this negotiated scheduling to address contention overhead and energy inefficiency, particularly for IoT-class devices sharing a WLAN with high-throughput traffic. The results showed reduced contention overhead and improved energy efficiency from TWT scheduling. The main advantage of this approach is a standardized, AP-coordinated primitive directly analogous to the coordination concept CoWiC-MAC generalizes, while a limitation is that TWT coordination is confined to the 802.11 ecosystem and does not extend to non-WiFi heterogeneous devices.

**Pan et al. (2020)** proposed CDA, a protocol coordinating data dissemination and aggregation in heterogeneous IoT networks using Cross-Technology Communication. The proposed approach uses CTC to run simultaneous dissemination (broadcast) and aggregation (collection) across WiFi and low-power heterogeneous radios in one coordinated process. It addresses the inefficiency of running separate, uncoordinated dissemination and aggregation passes per technology. The results showed reduced completion time and overhead compared to independent, technology-specific dissemination and aggregation. The main advantage of this approach is coordinated cross-technology efficiency, and it is directly relevant since co-authors Yan Pan and Shining Li also authored the CoWiC-MAC base paper, establishing direct research lineage; a limitation is that its coordination logic is tailored to dissemination/aggregation traffic rather than general MAC-level channel access.

**Shao et al. (2020)** presented PolarScout, a physical-layer technique making ZigBee communication resilient to WiFi interference via "shell-shaping." The proposed approach reshapes the ZigBee signal constellation so that WiFi-induced interference becomes easier to distinguish and correct at the receiver. It uses this PHY-layer modulation change to address ZigBee's vulnerability to interference without requiring any WiFi-side cooperation. The results showed substantially improved ZigBee packet reception under WiFi interference compared to standard O-QPSK ZigBee modulation. The main advantage of this approach is a purely local PHY fix needing no cross-technology signaling, while a limitation is that it improves resilience but does not coordinate channel access, so collisions under heavy WiFi load are only partially mitigated.

**Kim (2022)** proposed CTC-CSMA/CA, a mechanism enabling explicit WLAN–WPAN coexistence via Cross-Technology Communication. The proposed approach uses bidirectional CTC between WiFi and ZigBee to mimic CSMA/CA-style behavior, letting ZigBee explicitly request and receive feedback on channel resources, combined with beacon-synchronized low-duty-cycle ZigBee operation. It addresses the lack of explicit spectrum coordination in most existing coexistence methods, implemented on commodity WiFi and ZigBee hardware with no 802.11 standard modification. The results showed more accurate channel coordination and significantly improved ZigBee throughput versus conventional coexistence schemes. The main advantage of this approach is standard-compliant, hardware-unmodified explicit coordination closely mirroring CoWiC-MAC's philosophy, while a limitation is that scalability beyond a two-technology testbed was not demonstrated.

**Chen et al. (2022)** presented a scheme enabling global cooperation for heterogeneous networks via reliable concurrent Cross-Technology Communication. The proposed approach extends CTC from pairwise, point-to-point coordination to reliable, concurrent multi-node cross-technology communication. It addresses the scalability limitation of earlier CTC schemes when many heterogeneous devices must cooperate network-wide rather than in isolated pairs. The results showed that reliable concurrent CTC sustains cooperation performance as the number of participating heterogeneous devices scales, where pairwise CTC degrades. The main advantage of this approach is network-wide scalability absent from earlier point-to-point CTC coordination, while a limitation is the increased signaling and coordination complexity required to manage concurrency.

**Cheng et al. (2023)** proposed WibZig, a reliable, commodity-device-compatible physical-layer CTC technique using chip emulation in phase. The proposed approach extends WEBee-style PHY emulation into the phase domain, improving robustness over prior amplitude/frequency-domain emulation for WiFi-to-ZigBee communication. It addresses the reliability and hardware-compatibility limitations of earlier PHY-CTC emulation techniques. The results reported improved reliability and commodity-hardware compatibility relative to prior emulation approaches. The main advantage of this approach is that, authored by the same research group as CoWiC-MAC (Cheng, Li, Pan), it forms a direct technical precursor to the base paper's coordination mechanism, while a limitation is its focus on point-to-point PHY-CTC reliability rather than full MAC-level coordinated access across a heterogeneous network.

**Cheng et al. (2025)** proposed an enhanced Cross-Technology-Communication-based data dissemination method for Wireless Avionics Intra-Communications (WAIC) networks. The proposed approach builds on the authors' earlier PHY-CTC work to coordinate data dissemination across heterogeneous nodes in the WAIC aviation network context. It addresses limitations in dissemination effectiveness/efficiency of baseline CTC approaches in this heterogeneous setting. The results reported improved dissemination performance over baseline CTC methods. The main advantage of this approach is that it is the most recent work by the base paper's own authors, directly preceding and motivating CoWiC-MAC, while a limitation is that it is scoped to data-dissemination performance rather than a general-purpose coordinated MAC mechanism — the gap the base paper subsequently addresses.

## 5. Relevance of the Literature Survey to CoWiC-MAC

**Common problems addressed.** The surveyed papers converge on a single structural problem: WiFi and low-power, technologically incompatible radios (ZigBee/802.15.4, WPAN/IoT devices) must share the unlicensed 2.4 GHz ISM band, but neither a common MAC protocol nor mutual protocol awareness exists between them. This produces cross-technology interference, unpredictable packet loss, and inefficient, uncoordinated channel access — the same heterogeneous-coexistence problem that motivates CoWiC-MAC.

**Common techniques used.** Three technique families recur across the survey: (i) *passive/implicit coexistence* — sensing WiFi's temporal white space or activity to schedule the other technology's transmissions around it (Huang 2010; Liang 2010; Zhang & Shin 2011; Wang 2014; Shao 2020); (ii) *Cross-Technology Communication (CTC)* — enabling direct signaling between heterogeneous radios, evolving from physical-layer emulation (Li & He 2017; Cheng et al. 2023) to explicit coordination and reservation protocols (Yin et al. 2018; Kim 2022; Chen et al. 2022; Pan et al. 2020; Cheng et al. 2025); and (iii) *WiFi-native coordinated scheduling* — mechanisms built into the 802.11ax amendment itself (OFDMA, spatial reuse, Target Wake Time) that convert part of WiFi's own channel access from contention-based to AP-coordinated (Bellalta 2016; Deng 2017; Khorov 2019; Nurchis & Bellalta 2019), all resting on Bianchi's (2000) original DCF contention model and Jamieson & Balakrishnan's (2007) link-layer resilience techniques.

**Remaining limitations in existing approaches.** Passive/implicit schemes avoid interference reactively but cannot negotiate access precisely and scale poorly beyond two technologies. Early CTC work (WEBee-class emulation) achieves high-throughput cross-technology signaling but was largely demonstrated point-to-point, and pairwise CTC coordination (Yin 2018; Kim 2022) does not scale efficiently to many concurrent heterogeneous devices — a gap only partly closed by concurrent-CTC work (Chen et al. 2022). Meanwhile, 802.11ax's coordinated-access mechanisms (TWT, OFDMA) are powerful but operate strictly within the WiFi ecosystem and do not extend coordination to non-WiFi heterogeneous radios. No single prior scheme unifies *WiFi-native coordinated scheduling* with *cross-technology signaling* into one MAC mechanism explicitly centered on WiFi as the coordinating hub.

**Research background for CoWiC-MAC.** The base paper's own reference list shows a direct lineage: Pan et al. (2020, CDA) and Cheng et al. (2023, WibZig; 2025, WAIC-CTC) are prior work by the same author group, progressively building CTC-based coordination — first for data dissemination/aggregation, then for reliable PHY-layer emulation, then for heterogeneous avionics dissemination. CoWiC-MAC generalizes this trajectory into a full **coordinated, WiFi-centric MAC mechanism**, positioning WiFi (the higher-capability, infrastructure-anchored radio) as the active coordinator of channel access for heterogeneous low-power devices, rather than treating coexistence as passive avoidance or point-to-point signaling.

**What makes CoWiC-MAC relevant compared with prior work.** Unlike passive white-space or interference-tolerant coding schemes, CoWiC-MAC targets *active coordination* rather than tolerance. Unlike pairwise CTC schemes, it is explicitly WiFi-centric and MAC-level, aiming at network-wide coordination rather than single-link signaling. And unlike 802.11ax's TWT/OFDMA mechanisms, which coordinate only WiFi stations, CoWiC-MAC's WiFi-centric design extends coordinated medium access across heterogeneous device types — combining the scheduling discipline of 802.11ax-style coordination with the cross-technology reach of CTC, addressing the scalability and cross-technology-scope gaps identified across the survey.
